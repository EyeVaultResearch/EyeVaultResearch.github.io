Opening Eyes to Inclusion: An Insightful Analysis of Eye Tracking Datasets

Overview

Several calls have encouraged researchers to foster research ethics, reproducibility, and transparency in their empirical research. One method of achieving this is publishing datasets to promote open-source practices and encourage data reuse. Surprisingly, inclusivity in eye tracking data collection has been largely overlooked in the community. Most published models and prototypes lean heavily on existing datasets, claiming broad generalizability but do not consider the need for diverse data sources.

We critically evaluated 109 eye tracking datasets published or utilized in ETRA proceedings from ETRA 2000 to 2023, including adjunct materials. Our findings reveal that few published datasets include crucial information about factors impacting data generalizability, like sample size, ethnicity, age range, and eye colour.

This EyeVault is the culmination of our analysis, and provides details of the 109 datasets we evaluated. We aim to guide the eye tracking community toward a more diverse and inclusive approach to collecting eye tracking data.

What gets reported

Every dataset is a row, every participant attribute a column, sorted so the best-documented sit at the top. Gender and age are usually there. The eye characteristics that actually change how well a tracker performs — eyelid shape, pupil size, interpupillary distance — almost never are.
